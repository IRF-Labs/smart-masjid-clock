In short

  • The app works offline. Prayer times are calculated on your phone, for any place in the world.
  • There is no account, no advertising and no app backend. We never sell or rent your data; sending a report is your choice.
  • Your location, timetables, photos and settings stay on your phone unless you choose to share them. Uninstalling deletes the app's copies.
  • Four things can use the internet: online prayer times, which is off until you turn it on; city lists for a time zone, downloaded when you ask (or automatically, if you allow it); the masjid map and Google Maps short links, only when you tap them while adding a masjid, a profile's place or your own place, and the same map when you check the Qibla on it (section 5a); and Google ML Kit, the on-phone text reader for timetable photos, which sends Google diagnostics about itself.
  • On Google Play installs, Google Play may offer an optional review dialog. Feedback and bug reports leave through your own email app only when you send them.

1. What the app keeps on your phone

The app keeps its working data in private storage on your phone. We do not receive it. Android's cloud backup is switched off for this app, so Android does not automatically copy it to a backup service. Uninstalling the app, or clearing its storage in Android settings, deletes the app’s private copies, not exports saved elsewhere.

2. Location

The app asks for location only when you use a feature that needs it, such as "Use my current location". You can instead choose your city from the city lists and never grant location. Background location ("Allow all the time") is optional: after an explanation in the app, it lets the app switch to a saved mosque's timetable, or to one of your profiles, when you arrive, even when the app is closed. Arrival is detected on the phone by Android and Google Play services and, with background location allowed, by single position checks around each prayer time at your saved masjids near you and just before an auto-silence starts (also each time you open the app, with location allowed while in use); each takes one position, never follows you continuously, and stays on the phone. Qibla Direction, while it is open and only when location is allowed, asks for a fresh position (GPS, or the mobile network's or Wi-Fi's approximate position) when it opens and when you tap "Use my position now"; it uses it only on that screen, to work out the bearing and to show where you are on its map, and the app never saves or sends it (a mobile network or Wi-Fi position is found by the phone's location service, which may use the internet). My Masjid, Saved Masjids and Today, while open and only when location is allowed, ask for a fresh position in the same way (at most about once a minute) to show the distance and walk to your masjids from where you are; it is kept only in the phone's memory and used for up to five minutes (until a newer one comes), never saved or sent. Without one, distances are measured from your saved prayer place; My Masjid and Saved Masjids then say "from saved place". The app itself never sends your location anywhere, except the rounded position used by the optional online prayer times (section 4) and the map areas you view when you open the masjid map, a profile's place on the map or the Qibla's map (section 5a).

3. Profiles and Wi-Fi names

Profiles (for example Home, Office or Travel) are optional. A profile can switch on by days and times you choose, when the phone is connected to a Wi-Fi network you named, near a place you saved, or far from home. Android shows the connected Wi-Fi name only to apps with location permission. The app reads it on the phone, compares it with your profiles and keeps only the last name it read. Profiles, their places and Wi-Fi names are never sent anywhere and are deleted with the profile or the app.

4. Online prayer times (optional, off by default)

In Settings → Timetable → Automatic → Coordinates you can turn on Fetch prayer times online. The app explains it before you turn it on. When it is on, the app sends:

  • your location rounded to two decimal places (about 1 km), and
  • the calculation settings (method, Asr school and high-latitude rule)

over an encrypted connection (HTTPS) to api.aladhan.com, a prayer-times service run by a third party (AlAdhan) under its own terms, to get this month's and next month's prayer start times. It asks at most about once every 20 days per month and place. Nothing else is sent: no name, account, device ID, mosque, jamaat times, photos or files. The times are saved on the phone. If the service cannot be reached, or its times differ from the phone's own calculation by more than 15 minutes, the phone's calculation is used. Turning the switch off stops all requests.

5. City lists (downloaded when you ask)

The app includes the biggest cities of every time zone. Every other city comes in one list per time zone, downloaded from the app's website only when you tap Download, or, if you turn on Download automatically when the phone is in a new time zone after an explanation in the app, when your phone is in a time zone whose list is not on the phone yet. The request asks for that time zone's file over HTTPS and sends nothing else: no location, settings, account or device ID. Like any web request, the website sees which time zone's list is asked for and your internet address. Each list is checked against a checksum built into the app before it is used. Only the most recently used lists are kept (3, 5 or 10, your choice), and in Settings → City lists & storage you can mark lists to keep or remove any of them. The website's host may keep ordinary access logs (the file asked for and the internet address) under its own terms; we do not use them, and uninstalling the app does not delete them.

5a. Masjid map (only when you tap it)

When adding or editing a masjid, for a profile's place, and when adding your own place (City search → Add your own place), Pick on the map opens an OpenStreetMap map. The same map is used for the Qibla check: on Qibla Direction, Check on a map shows the line from your position (or saved place) towards the Kaaba on it. Its map pictures (tiles) are downloaded from OpenStreetMap's tile servers, so those servers see which map areas you view (around the masjid, your place or where the Qibla is checked), your internet address and the app's name; the pictures are kept in the app's cache on the phone. OpenStreetMap is run by the OpenStreetMap Foundation under its own terms (osmfoundation.org). Or paste a Google Maps link reads the coordinates on the phone; only a short link (maps.app.goo.gl) is opened, over HTTPS and only on Google's own addresses, to find the full link it points to, so Google sees that link and your internet address, under Google's terms. Open in Google Maps (on the map) opens the Google Maps app, or the browser, at the point shown, so Google sees that point, and only when you tap it. A place you share from Google Maps (Share → Masjid Companion) is kept in memory (its name and link, never saved) until you use it or set it aside. If a map is open here it is offered there, and nothing is opened or sent for it until you tap Use it. If no map is open, the app reads it as soon as you share it: a Google short link is opened as described above to find its position (Google sees that link and your internet address, under Google's terms) and Add Masjid opens with the name and position filled in; the name is read from the shared text on the phone. Nothing else is sent: no account, masjid name, times, Qibla bearing or files. Your places (the names and positions you save in the city search) stay on the phone; they are in a backup file only when you make one.

5b. Find Masjids Nearby (only when you tap it)

Find Masjids Nearby (on My Masjid and Saved Masjids) looks up masjids on OpenStreetMap, only when you tap it or Try again. The app divides the map into squares of about 11 km and asks the Overpass service in one request: overpass-api.de first (when it is busy the app asks it again, up to three times with a short wait), and when that fails for any reason (busy, no answer in time, an error or no connection) the backup server overpass.private.coffee once, with the same box; the whole search ends within 90 seconds. It asks for the masjids in the squares your 3 km circle touches that are not already saved on the phone. Only that box (an area of up to about 25 km across) is sent, never your position, name or anything else (the same box is sent again when the service is busy, and to the backup server when the first fails); the service also sees your internet address and the app's name, under OpenStreetMap's and the operator's own terms. The answers are kept on the phone for 30 days so the same area is not asked for again; a search whose squares are all saved sends nothing; the answers are kept in the app's own files, not in backups, and clearing the app's storage deletes them. A masjid from the map has no jamaat times and never drives an alarm or silence until you add it and enter its times. Search in Google Maps (at the top of Find Masjids Nearby) opens the Google Maps app, or another app that opens map links, searching for “mosque”; the app sends nothing in it (Google Maps uses its own location, under Google's terms). See in Google Maps (on each masjid in the list) opens Google Maps, or the browser, in satellite view at that masjid's point, so Google sees that one point (never your position) and your internet address. Neither opens unless you tap it.

6. Reading timetable photos

Text in timetable photos is read on the phone with Google ML Kit's built-in text recognizer. Photos, PDFs and the text read from them are never uploaded. Google ML Kit sends Google diagnostics and usage information about the ML Kit library itself (for example device and app version, performance and errors), which Google says can include a per-installation identifier. This does not include your photos, the text read or your location. See Google's ML Kit data disclosure.

7. Crash reports and feedback

Settings → Help & feedback → Crash reports offers three choices. Ask me (the default) saves a report on the phone and asks on a later app opening whether you want to send it. Always save keeps it for you to send when you choose. Off records nothing. Reports contain the app and Android version, phone model, crash time and code locations, without error messages, prayer times, places, mosque names or files. Nothing is uploaded automatically. An unsent crash report is deleted after 14 days; you can delete it sooner.

Feedback and bug reports

Help & feedback sends feedback or bug reports through your own email app to the developer only when you choose Send. A bug report's diagnostics text is shown in full first: app and Android version, phone model, yes/no permission and feature states, recent reasons the app was closed, and the last crash report. Location, place, mosque, profile and Wi-Fi names, recordings and timetable are excluded. Alarm history and prayer settings (calculation method, time zone and jamaat rules) are added only if you tick them. Screenshots are attached only if you pick them. The last 200 alarm events behind “Why didn't my alarm ring?” stay on the phone unless you choose to include history in a report.

Temporary report files on the phone are deleted after a day. Emails arrive in the developer's mailbox and are deleted once the problem is resolved, at the latest after 90 days; contact us to delete yours sooner.

Google Play reviews

For Google Play installs, the app may offer Google's optional review dialog after a week of successful alarm use, with no recent missed alarm or crash. No alarm history, timetable, location or crash report is attached to that request. Reviews you submit are handled by Google Play under its own terms. Attempts are limited to once per 90 days; Google may not show the dialog. Help & feedback keeps a direct store link.

8. Permissions

  • Exact alarms, full-screen alerts, notifications, wake lock, starting after a restart: ring prayer alarms on time, also after the phone restarts.
  • Do Not Disturb access: silence the phone during prayers. On Android 15 and later this is the app's own Do Not Disturb mode, which never turns off a mode of yours.
  • Foreground service: keep the alarm schedule running on phones that close background apps, and play the azaan.
  • Battery settings: ask Android not to delay the alarms.
  • Location (optional) and Wi-Fi state: as described in sections 2 and 3.
  • Internet: only for the optional online prayer times, city-list downloads, Find Masjids Nearby, the masjid map (also the Qibla's map) and Google Maps short links (when you tap them or share a place from Google Maps), and Google ML Kit's diagnostics.

The app does not use the camera permission (photos come from your camera app), contacts, microphone, messages or advertising ID.

9. Sharing

Apart from ML Kit's diagnostics, the optional online prayer times and city-list downloads, and the masjid map (also used for the Qibla check), Find Masjids Nearby (section 5b, with its Search in Google Maps and See in Google Maps buttons) and Google Maps short links when you tap them or share a place from Google Maps (section 5a), the app shares nothing by itself. Something leaves the phone only when you send it yourself, for example a crash report, bug report or feedback through your email app, or a board photo opened in another app. We do not sell, rent or trade data, and there is no advertising or tracking.

10. Security

Data is kept in the app's private storage, which other apps cannot read. The app has no backend or automatic report upload. Reports you choose to email are held in the developer's mailbox as described in section 7. Online prayer times, city lists, nearby-masjid lookups, the masjid map's tiles, Google Maps short links and ML Kit's diagnostics travel over encrypted connections (HTTPS), and each downloaded city list is checked before use.

11. Keeping and deleting data

Data stays on your phone until you delete it. In the app you can delete a profile, a saved mosque, a crash report, imported prayer start times or the jamaat times learned for a place; in Settings → City lists & storage you can remove downloaded city lists, saved online prayer times and saved-masjid calculations; you can turn off online prayer times and automatic downloads at any time, or delete everything by uninstalling the app or clearing its storage in Android settings. Unsent crash reports expire after 14 days and temporary report files after a day. If you send an email, the developer deletes it once resolved, within 90 days at the latest; you can request earlier deletion. Data that Google ML Kit, AlAdhan, OpenStreetMap (map tiles and nearby-masjid lookups) or Google (a short link) receive is handled under their own policies. The masjid map's tiles are kept in the app's cache, which Android may clear and which Settings → Apps → Storage → Clear cache deletes.

12. Your choices and rights

You decide what the app may use: location, background location, Do Not Disturb access, online prayer times, city-list downloads and crash reports can each be refused or turned off without losing the rest of the app. Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR in the European Union and United Kingdom, or privacy laws in other countries and US states), you may have rights to access, correct or delete personal data. Because the app keeps your data only on your phone, you can exercise these rights directly in the app or by uninstalling it. For questions, contact us (section 15).

13. Users around the world

The app can be used in any country. Your data is processed on your own phone. If you turn on online prayer times, the rounded location and calculation settings are sent to AlAdhan's service; city lists come from the app's website; the masjid map's tiles and the nearby-masjid listings come from OpenStreetMap and a Google Maps short link is opened at Google, when you tap them or share a place from Google Maps; and Google ML Kit's diagnostics are sent to Google; these services may process data in other countries under their own terms.

14. Children

Masjid Companion: Salah Times is a general-audience prayer clock. It does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children.
